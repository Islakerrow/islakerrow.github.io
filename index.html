Cross When Clear

Every afternoon at a quarter past three, a little girl crosses Lorna Webb’s road.

For eleven years Lorna has been the lollipop lady outside St Anne’s Primary in Gloucester. She knows every child. So she notices the girl in the red jumper with a felt fox on her bag, and the different woman who meets her every day. Then the school tells her they have no such pupil.

She took the job to pay off a debt. She didn’t know she was replacing someone.

Three weeks of house-sitting on a remote Cornish headland. The dressing gown left out for her fits exactly. The neighbour calls her Claire. Then, at two o’clock one morning, the old cream telephone at the foot of the stairs begins to ring.

Under the Lining

There’s a note sewn into the wedding dress. It’s dated June 1998.

For twenty-two years, Nell has altered other people’s dresses in her Bath workroom and never asked why. Then she opens a seam on the Lyall family’s gown and finds a strip of calico hidden beneath the lining. The wedding is nine days away.
